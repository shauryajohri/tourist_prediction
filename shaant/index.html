The prediction comes from a gradient boosted classifier trained on seven years of daily records for 29 destinations. It learns from the month, the day of the week, whether the date is a public holiday, and how busy that destination has been on that calendar day in the past.

Normally a model like that needs Python and a terminal. This one does not. Every answer it can give was worked out ahead of time and packed into the page, so the whole thing runs in your browser with nothing to install and nothing to wait for.

What it cannot do yet

The dataset behind this is sample data, not real gate counts. Every destination in it moves almost identically, so the model can tell a busy month from a quiet one but cannot yet tell Nainital apart from Auli. Real visitor records would fix that without changing a line of the page you are looking at.

Questions

The things people ask before they book.

When is the best time to go?
March to April and mid September to mid October are the clear winners. Skies are open, the temperature is kind, and the roads behave. June to August is monsoon, which means landslides and blocked roads, though it also means cheap rooms and empty viewpoints if you can be flexible.
Is a long weekend really that bad?
Yes. On the last big one, police only let cars into Nainital through the Rusi bypass if they could show a confirmed hotel booking, and the queue to get in ran two kilometres.
Should I just go somewhere quieter instead?
Often, yes. Chopta, Munsiyari and Mukteshwar sit a few hours further out and stay calm in the same weeks that Mall Road fills up.
What about winter?
December and January bring snow and closed roads in the higher reaches. Go, but keep a spare day in the plan so a blocked pass does not end the trip.
How accurate is this?
Honest answer: it reads the season well and it cannot yet read the destination. The numbers come from sample data while real records are added. Use it to pick a month and a weekday, not to count heads.
